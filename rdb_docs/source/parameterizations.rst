.. _parameterizations:

------------------
Parameterizations
------------------

.. contents::
   :local:

A hydrostatic ocean model at 1–100 km resolution cannot resolve the
processes that do most of the mixing: the turbulent boundary layer, breaking
internal waves, convective plumes, and — depending on resolution — the
mesoscale eddy field. Those have to be parameterized.

This page gives the **defining formula** for each closure as it is actually
implemented, so you can tell what the model is doing. It is deliberately not
a knob-by-knob table:

.. note::

   For the complete list of knobs, their defaults, and which closure is
   active in which configuration, see ``docs/CLOSURE_MATRIX.md`` in the
   repository — it is drift-checked against the source by
   ``tools/check_closure_matrix.py``, so it cannot silently go stale. The
   full generated knob-to-default map is ``docs/generated_nml_knobs.md``,
   regenerated by the ``rdb_nml_doc`` binary. Neither is duplicated here.

Almost every closure below defaults **off**, which means enabling nothing
reproduces earlier results bit-for-bit. The two on-by-default exceptions
are the PP81 interior closure with its KPP overlay, and the ALE remap.


Vertical mixing
===============

How the pieces combine
----------------------

Vertical mixing is not one scheme but a stack of contributors that each
write into three interface-centred diffusivity fields — :math:`k_v` for
momentum, :math:`k_t` for temperature, :math:`k_s` for salinity and every
passive tracer. The order is fixed and the combination rule differs per
contributor:

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 8 34 58

   * - \#
     - Contributor
     - Rule
   * - 1
     - PP81 interior
     - **Assign** :math:`k_v`, :math:`k_t`. Assignment, not accumulation,
       is what makes the per-stage chain idempotent.
   * - 2
     - KPP overlay **xor** EPBL
     - KPP: ``max``. EPBL: **add** (or ``max``).
   * - 3
     - kappa-shear
     - Additive.
   * - 4
     - Tidal mixing
     - Additive.
   * - 5
     - Convective adjustment
     - ``max``, and only *below* the active boundary layer.
   * - 6
     - ``vmix_split_kd_heat_salt``
     - Not a contributor — **derives** :math:`k_s` from :math:`k_t`.
   * - 7
     - ``vmix_assemble``
     - The single gate: guards, floors, ceilings, smoothing.

Two structural rules follow from that table and are worth internalising
before adding anything:

**No contributor applies its own floor or clip.** That is
``vmix_assemble``'s job, and it is the only place it happens. A closure
that floors its own output defeats the gate.

**Step 6 must stay last before the gate.** A new :math:`k_v`/:math:`k_t`
contributor inserted *after* the split silently never reaches :math:`k_s`,
and the salinity field quietly stops seeing it.

The split rule itself is simple in the default case: with double diffusion
off, :math:`k_s \equiv k_t` exactly. With ``&ocean_ddiff_nml enable``, it
becomes MOM6's asymmetric pair — :math:`K_{\mathrm{salt}} = K_{\mathrm{int}}
+ K^{\mathrm{extra}}_{S}` and :math:`K_{\mathrm{heat}} = K_{\mathrm{int}} +
K^{\mathrm{extra}}_{T}` — branching on the *signed* comparison of
:math:`\alpha \Delta T` against :math:`\beta \Delta S` to distinguish salt
fingering from diffusive convection.

PP81 — the interior closure
---------------------------

Pacanowski & Philander (1981). Richardson-number-dependent diffusivity,
on by default via ``&ocean_vmix_nml use_closure``.

With :math:`N^2` the buoyancy frequency, :math:`S^2` the squared shear and
:math:`\mathrm{Ri} = N^2/S^2`:

.. math::
   :label: eq-pp81

   \nu \;=\; \nu_{bg} + \frac{\nu_0}{\bigl(1 + \alpha \max(\mathrm{Ri},0)\bigr)^2} ,
   \qquad
   \kappa \;=\; \kappa_{bg} + \frac{\nu_0}{\bigl(1 + \alpha \max(\mathrm{Ri},0)\bigr)^3} .

The exponents differ — 2 for momentum, 3 for tracers — which is what makes
the turbulent Prandtl number increase with stratification, as observed.
Defaults: :math:`\nu_0 = 10^{-2}`, :math:`\nu_{bg} = 10^{-4}`,
:math:`\kappa_{bg} = 10^{-5}\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}`, :math:`\alpha = 5`.

Note the :math:`\max(\mathrm{Ri}, 0)` clip: an unstable column
(:math:`\mathrm{Ri} < 0`) *saturates* the coefficients at
:math:`\nu_{bg} + \nu_0` rather than diverging. Unstable columns are the
job of convective adjustment, not of PP81.

KPP — the boundary-layer overlay
--------------------------------

On by default (``&ocean_vmix_nml use_kpp``), and mutually exclusive with
EPBL. It is an **overlay on top of PP81**, not a standalone scheme.

**Boundary-layer depth.** From a bulk Richardson number referenced to the
surface layer:

.. math::

   \mathrm{Ri}_b(d) \;=\;
     \frac{\bigl(B_{\mathrm{ref}} - B(d)\bigr)\,(d - d_{\mathrm{ref}})}
          {\bigl| \mathbf{V}(d) - \mathbf{V}_{\mathrm{ref}} \bigr|^2
           + V_t^2(d)} ,

with the turbulent-velocity-shear term

.. math::

   V_t^2(d) \;=\; \frac{c_{vt2}}{\mathrm{Ri}_c} \, d \, N(d) \, w_s .

The boundary-layer depth :math:`h_b` is where :math:`\mathrm{Ri}_b` first
reaches ``kpp_ri_crit`` (default 0.3), linearly interpolated between layer
centres; if it never does, :math:`h_b` is the full column.

**Diffusivity.** With :math:`\sigma = d/h_b`,

.. math::
   :label: eq-kpp-shape

   G(\sigma) \;=\; \sigma \, (1 - \sigma)^2 ,
   \qquad
   K \;=\; h_b \, w_s \, G(\sigma) ,

merged into the interior by ``max``, so outside the boundary layer PP81
carries through untouched. The velocity scale is

.. math::

   u_* = \sqrt{|\tau|/\rho_0} ,
   \quad
   w_* = \bigl( \max(0, -B_0) \, h_b \bigr)^{1/3} ,
   \quad
   w_s = \sqrt{u_*^2 + w_*^2} .

**Non-local transport.** Where the surface buoyancy flux is destabilising,
a counter-gradient term redistributes the surface flux through the boundary
layer:

.. math::

   \gamma_X \;=\; C_s \, G(\sigma) \, F_X ,
   \qquad
   \frac{\partial (h\,X)}{\partial t}
     \;\mathrel{+}=\; \Delta t \,
       \bigl( \gamma_{X,k+1} - \gamma_{X,k} \bigr) ,

with :math:`C_s` = ``kpp_cs_nonlocal``, default 6.3.

.. warning::

   **This is a reduced KPP, and the omissions are substantive.** Do not
   assume the Large, McWilliams & Doney (1994) scheme in full. Absent:

   * the Monin-Obukhov length and the stability functions
     :math:`\phi_m, \phi_s` — :math:`w_s` is the bare
     :math:`\sqrt{u_*^2 + w_*^2}` with no :math:`\zeta = d/L` dependence;
   * separate momentum and scalar velocity scales — :math:`w_m \equiv w_s`,
     so the turbulent Prandtl number is **identically one** inside the
     boundary layer;
   * entrainment matching of :math:`\partial K/\partial z` at
     :math:`h_b` — the ``max`` merge is the entire interface treatment;
   * Langmuir / Stokes enhancement.

   One consequence follows directly: under net surface *heating*
   (:math:`B_0 > 0`), :math:`w_* \equiv 0` and the shortwave-method
   correction is a numerical no-op. If boundary-layer fidelity is what
   your configuration depends on, use EPBL.

EPBL — energetics boundary layer
--------------------------------

Reichl & Hallberg (2018), ``&ocean_epbl_nml enable``, default off.
Mutually exclusive with KPP; PP81 and the background still run underneath.

EPBL replaces "diagnose a depth, then impose a profile" with a **turbulent
kinetic energy budget**. Two reservoirs are filled per column per step:

.. math::

   \mathrm{TKE}_{\mathrm{mech}} = m_* \, \rho_0 \, u_*^3 \, \Delta t ,
   \qquad
   \mathrm{TKE}_{\mathrm{conv}} \;\text{from the surface buoyancy flux} ,

combined as :math:`\mathrm{TKE}_{\mathrm{tot}} = \mathrm{TKE}_{\mathrm{mech}}
+ n_*\,\mathrm{TKE}_{\mathrm{conv}}`. A stabilising buoyancy flux is instead
*charged against* the mechanical reservoir rather than seeding the
convective one.

The wind-energy coefficient :math:`m_*` is itself a closure, selected by
``mstar_scheme`` (accepted: ``constant``, ``om4`` — the default — and
``rh18``); the OM4 form scales it with the Ekman-to-boundary-layer depth
ratio.

The first-guess diffusivity is a mixing-length closure

.. math::

   K_d \;=\; \kappa \, v_* \, L ,
   \qquad
   L \;=\; \frac{h\,\Lambda\,v_*}
                {C_{Ek} \, |f| \, h \, \Lambda + v_*} ,
   \qquad
   v_* \;\propto\; \left( \frac{\mathrm{TKE}}{\rho_0 \Delta t} \right)^{1/3} ,

with :math:`L` the harmonic rolloff between a shear length and an Ekman
length, and :math:`\Lambda` a shape factor decaying to the mixed-layer base.

**But the guess is only a guess.** What makes this scheme *energetic* is
that the potential-energy cost of mixing at each interface is computed in
closed form and compared against the TKE available. If mixing at
:math:`K_d` would cost more than there is, the energy-limited value is used
instead. **The mixed-layer depth is where the energy runs out** — it is an
outcome, not a diagnosis. ``mld_iteration`` (default ``.true.``)
root-finds it.

Langmuir turbulence is available via ``use_lt`` (default off), with the wave
state derived from :math:`u_*` alone through a COARE 3.5 inversion. There is
no prognostic wave model and no Stokes-drift input field.

Scope limits stated in the source: surface boundary layer **only** — no
bottom-boundary EPBL — and no mean-kinetic-energy-to-TKE conversion, which
is why the energy solve is closed-form rather than iterative.

kappa-shear — interior shear turbulence
---------------------------------------

Jackson, Hallberg & Legg (2008), ``&ocean_kappa_shear_nml enable``, default
off. Genuinely **prognostic**: two coupled steady-state
diffusion-reaction equations per column, solved as alternating tridiagonal
sweeps to Picard convergence inside an adaptive substep loop.

The source function is non-zero only where the gradient Richardson number
falls below critical:

.. math::
   :label: eq-kshear-src

   \kappa_{\mathrm{src}} \;=\;
     \frac{2 \, a \, \sqrt{S^2}\,
           \bigl( \mathrm{Ri}_c S^2 - N^2 \bigr)}
          {\mathrm{Ri}_c S^2 + c_{\mathrm{curv}} N^2} ,
   \qquad N^2 < \mathrm{Ri}_c S^2 .

The turbulent kinetic energy :math:`Q` and the diffusivity :math:`\kappa`
then satisfy

.. math::

   \frac{\partial}{\partial z}
     \Bigl[ (\kappa + \kappa_0) \frac{\partial Q}{\partial z} \Bigr]
   + (\kappa + \kappa_0) S^2
   - Q \left[ \sqrt{c_N^2 N^2 + c_S^2 S^2} + N^2 \frac{\kappa}{Q} \right]
   &= 0 , \\[6pt]
   \frac{\partial^2 \kappa}{\partial z^2}
   - \frac{\kappa}{L_d^2} + \kappa_{\mathrm{src}} &= 0 ,
   \qquad
   \frac{1}{L_d^2} = \frac{N^2/\lambda^2 + f^2}{Q} + \frac{1}{L_z^2} .

Shear produces :math:`Q` against the interior diffusivity; :math:`Q` decays
at :math:`\sqrt{c_N^2 N^2 + c_S^2 S^2}` and loses energy to buoyancy. The
merge is **additive**.

An opt-in vertex form (``at_vertex``, MOM6 ``VERTEX_SHEAR``) solves at cell
corners on the native face velocities and routes the viscosity corner-to-face
into the momentum solve directly, suppressing the cell-centred
:math:`k_v` merge.

Tidal mixing
------------

St Laurent et al. (2002) / Simmons et al. (2004),
``&ocean_tidal_mixing_nml enable``, default off. Internal tides break near
rough topography and mix the deep ocean; this parameterizes that as a
bottom-intensified diffusivity:

.. math::
   :label: eq-tidal

   K_d(z) \;=\;
     \frac{q \, \Gamma \, E(x,y) \, F(z)}
          {\rho_0 \left( N^2 + \Omega^2 \right)} ,
   \qquad
   F(z) \;=\; \frac{e^{-z/\zeta}}{\zeta \bigl( 1 - e^{-H/\zeta} \bigr)} ,

with :math:`z` the height above the bed. :math:`q` is the fraction of
internal-tide energy dissipated locally (``gamma``, default 0.3333),
:math:`\Gamma` the mixing efficiency (``mu``, default 0.2), :math:`\zeta`
the vertical decay scale (``zeta``, default 500 m), and :math:`E(x,y)` the
energy flux into internal tides.

:math:`F(z)` is normalised so that the per-layer deposition sums to exactly
:math:`q\Gamma E` — the implementation is written as a flux-bookkeeping
sweep precisely to make that exact. The merge is additive.

Documented divergences from MOM6: the bed and surface layers are excluded
from deposition; the ``kd_max`` clamp **discards** the clipped power rather
than re-injecting it into the layer above, so the closure is not
energy-conserving under clipping; and there is no bottom-boundary-layer
roughness override of :math:`N^2`.

Convective adjustment
---------------------

``&ocean_conv_nml enable``, default off. The bluntest instrument here, and
the right one for a statically unstable column:

.. math::

   N^2 < \texttt{n2\_thresh}
   \quad\Longrightarrow\quad
   k_t \leftarrow \max(k_t, K_{\mathrm{conv}}) ,
   \quad
   k_v \leftarrow \max(k_v, \mathrm{Pr}_{\mathrm{conv}} K_{\mathrm{conv}}) ,

with ``kd_conv`` defaulting to :math:`1\ \mathrm{m^2\,s^{-1}}` — five orders
above a typical interior value, which is the point: mix it away fast.

Two details matter. It is a ``max`` contributor, never an overwrite. And it
is masked out *above* the active boundary layer, whose depth comes from
whichever of KPP or EPBL is running — convection inside the boundary layer
is that scheme's business.

It writes :math:`k_v` and :math:`k_t` only, **never** :math:`k_s`. Because
it runs before the split, the default :math:`k_s := k_t` carries the
convective value to salinity anyway.

Background mixing
-----------------

The scalar floors that ``vmix_assemble`` applies can be replaced by one of
two depth- or latitude-dependent schemes. They are **mutually exclusive** —
enabling both fails loud at configure, matching MOM6's one-background-scheme
rule — and both default off, so the default path is bit-identical.

**Bryan & Lewis (1979)**, ``&ocean_vmix_nml bkgnd_profile``, a depth
profile that puts weak mixing in the thermocline and stronger mixing in the
abyss:

.. math::

   K_{bg}(z) \;=\; K_{\mathrm{sfc}}
     + \left( K_{\mathrm{deep}} - K_{\mathrm{sfc}} \right)
       \left[ \frac{1}{2}
              + \frac{1}{\pi} \arctan\!\left( \frac{z - z_0}{\Delta} \right)
       \right] .

Depth is accumulated from the *current* layer thicknesses, so the profile
tracks the moving free surface under any vertical coordinate.

**Henyey, Wright & Flatté (1986)**, ``&ocean_vmix_nml bkgnd_henyey``, a
latitude factor that scales the scalar tracer floor:

.. math::

   L(\varphi) \;=\;
     \frac{|\sin\varphi| \;
           \mathrm{arccosh}\!\left(
             \dfrac{N_0/2\Omega}{\max(|\sin\varphi|, \epsilon)} \right)}
          {\sin 30^\circ \;
           \mathrm{arccosh}\!\left(
             \dfrac{N_0/2\Omega}{\sin 30^\circ} \right)} ,

normalised so that :math:`L(30^\circ) = 1` exactly and
:math:`L(0^\circ) = 0` exactly. The floor becomes
:math:`\max(K_{\min}, K_{bg} \cdot L(\varphi))`; the momentum floor stays the
plain scalar.

This is the **constant-**:math:`N_0` **simplification** of Harrison &
Hallberg (2008): the in-situ stratification is replaced by a fixed
reference, so :math:`L` is a pure function of latitude with no per-step
:math:`N` dependence. It fails loud on a Cartesian grid, where every column
would be treated as equatorial.

Vertical diffusion and the implicit folds
-----------------------------------------

The diffusivities are applied by a **backward-Euler tridiagonal solve per
column**, unconditionally stable and therefore imposing no time-step
restriction however thin the layers get:

.. math::

   \left( 1 + \alpha_k + \beta_k \right) T_k^{n+1}
     - \alpha_k T_{k+1}^{n+1} - \beta_k T_{k-1}^{n+1}
     \;=\; T_k^{n} ,

.. math::

   \alpha_k = \frac{\Delta t \, K}{h_k \, \delta z_{k+1}} ,
   \qquad
   \beta_k = \frac{\Delta t \, K}{h_k \, \delta z_{k}} ,
   \qquad
   \delta z_k = \tfrac{1}{2}\left( h_{k-1} + h_k \right) .

Vanishing layers are decoupled as identity rows, so their tracer mass is
preserved exactly. The matrix is factorised once per stage and reused for
every registered tracer.

Two optional folds, both in ``&ocean_vdiff_nml`` and both default off, put
the boundary stresses **inside** this solve rather than applying them
explicitly beforehand:

``implicit_stress``
   Wind stress becomes a Neumann right-hand-side term at :math:`k = N_z`,
   :math:`\mathrm{RHS} \mathrel{+}= \Delta t \,\tau/(\rho_0 h_{N_z})`. In
   the zero-interior-coupling limit this reduces exactly to the explicit
   update.

``implicit_drag``
   Bottom drag becomes a diagonal term at :math:`k = 1`,
   :math:`b_1 \leftarrow 1 + \alpha_1 + \Delta t \lambda_{\mathrm{bot}}`. A
   sink becomes a *positive* diagonal addition, so the amplification factor
   is bounded by one for any :math:`h/\Delta t`.

Both exist to kill the thin-layer CFL blow-up that explicit stress and drag
suffer when a z\*-layer pinches out near the bed.

Bottom drag
-----------

``&ocean_bdrag_nml form``, accepted ``quadratic`` (the default) or
``linear``. Note that both coefficients default to zero, so drag is *off*
out of the box until you set one.

.. math::

   \text{linear:} \quad
     \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} \Bigg|_{k=1} = -\, r \, u_1 ,
   \qquad
   \text{quadratic:} \quad
     \frac{\partial u}{\partial t} \Bigg|_{k=1}
       = -\, \frac{C_d \, |\mathbf{U}| \, u_1}{h_1} ,

with :math:`|\mathbf{U}| = \sqrt{u^2 + \bar{v}^2}` and :math:`v`
four-point-averaged onto the u-face.

Both have an **HBBL-distributed** mode (``hbbl > 0``) that spreads the drag
across every layer overlapping the bottom ``hbbl`` metres, weighted by each
layer's fractional coverage, so that the column integral recovers the bulk
drag. The quadratic distributed form first builds a BBL-mean velocity,
floors its magnitude at ``bg_vel``, and applies the resulting drag
proportionally.

Every branch is multiplied by the face wet-mask, so wall faces get exactly
zero.


Lateral closures
================

Horizontal viscosity
--------------------

``&ocean_hvisc_nml``. Two independent pieces that **compose** — a
Laplacian and a biharmonic — plus the coefficient closures that set them.

The Laplacian can be a plain velocity Laplacian (the default) or, with
``stress_tensor = .true.``, a proper stress-tensor form built from the two
strain rates

.. math::

   D_T = \frac{\partial u}{\partial x} - \frac{\partial v}{\partial y}
   \quad \text{(tension, at cell centres)} ,
   \qquad
   D_S = \frac{\partial v}{\partial x} + \frac{\partial u}{\partial y}
   \quad \text{(shear, at corners)} ,

with stresses :math:`\sigma_{xx} = A \, D_T \, h` at centres and
:math:`\sigma_{xy} = A \, D_S \, h \cdot \mathrm{slip}` at corners. On a
uniform grid with uniform thickness and all-wet cells this reduces to
:math:`A\nabla^2\mathbf{u}` to round-off. The slip factor is set by
``no_slip`` (default ``.false.``, i.e. **free-slip**).

The biharmonic is :math:`-\nu_4 \nabla^4 \mathbf{u}`, applied as two passes
of :math:`\nabla^2`. It composes with all three harmonic operators, matching
MOM6 — selecting the stress tensor does *not* disable it, and anisotropic
viscosity plus biharmonic is a legal combination.

.. note::

   A documented fidelity divergence: the composition happens at the
   *tendency* level, so under ``stress_tensor`` the harmonic part is
   momentum-conserving and coast-masked while the biharmonic part is
   neither. MOM6 sums both into one stress tensor before differencing.

**CFL clamps.** Explicit viscosity has a forward-Euler stability limit and
the code enforces it per face:

.. math::

   \nu_{\max} \;=\;
     \frac{c_{\mathrm{bound}}}{8 \, \Delta t
       \left( \Delta x^{-2} + \Delta y^{-2} \right)} ,
   \qquad
   \nu_{4,\max} \;=\;
     \frac{2 \, c_{\mathrm{bound}}}
          {\Delta t \left[ (\pi/\Delta x)^2 + (\pi/\Delta y)^2 \right]^2} .

.. warning::

   The biharmonic CFL limit is a hard ceiling that a configuration can
   run into: with forward-Euler time stepping it caps :math:`\nu_4` around
   :math:`3\times10^{8}`, so MOM6's :math:`10^{9}`–:math:`10^{10}` values
   are **not reachable explicitly**. If you copy a :math:`\nu_4` from a
   MOM6 configuration and the clamp silently binds, you are running with
   less dissipation than you asked for.

Coefficient closures
--------------------

``&ocean_hvisc_nml lateral_closure`` selects the flow-aware coefficient.
Accepted: ``none`` (the default), ``leith``, ``smagorinsky`` / ``smag``,
``biharmonic``, ``leith_biharm``.

**Smagorinsky.** Viscosity from the local deformation rate:

.. math::

   A_h \;=\; \left( C_S \, \Delta x \right)^2 \,
             \sqrt{D_T^2 + D_S^2} ,

with :math:`C_S` = ``c_smag``, default 0.15. The biharmonic companion,
``smag_ah`` (default off):

.. math::

   A_4 \;=\; C_b \, L^4 \, \sqrt{D_T^2 + D_S^2} ,
   \qquad
   L^2 = \frac{2 \Delta x^2 \Delta y^2}{\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2} ,

with :math:`C_b` = ``smag_bi_const``, default 0.06. Deliberately *not*
resolution-scaled — a :math:`k^4` operator already spares the resolved
scales.

**Leith.** Viscosity from the vorticity gradient instead of the strain,
which targets enstrophy rather than energy:

.. math::

   A_h \;=\; \left( C_L \, \Delta x \right)^3 \, \left| \nabla \zeta \right| ,

with :math:`C_L` = ``c_leith``, default 1.0. The biharmonic companion uses
:math:`\left| \nabla^2 \zeta \right|` with a :math:`\pi^{-6}` scaling, and
its coefficient defaults to zero — meaning it is **inert unless you set
it**.

MEKE — mesoscale eddy kinetic energy
------------------------------------

``&ocean_meke_nml enable``, default off; requires GM. A prognostic 2D eddy
energy field :math:`E`, carried across restarts, which lets an
eddy-permitting but not eddy-resolving grid remember how energetic its
unresolved eddy field is.

.. math::

   \frac{\partial E}{\partial t}
     \;=\; \underbrace{\gamma_{GM} \frac{\mathcal{S}_{GM}}{M}}_{\text{GM PE release}}
       \;-\; \underbrace{\gamma_{fr} \frac{\mathcal{D}_{KE}}{M}}_{\text{resolved KE dissipation}}
       \;+\; \mathcal{S}_{bg}
       \;-\; \underbrace{\lambda_{\mathrm{drag}} E}_{\text{bottom drag}}
       \;+\; \nabla \!\cdot\! \left( \kappa_E \nabla E \right)
       \;-\; \nabla \!\cdot\! \left( \mathbf{U} E \right) ,

Strang-split once per thermodynamic step, with the bottom-drag sink taken
implicitly in two half-steps around the lateral terms so it cannot go
unstable.

:math:`E` feeds back two ways:

**Diffusivity.** :math:`K_h = C_{Kh} \sqrt{2 \gamma_t^2 E} \, L_{\mathrm{mix}}`,
fed into the GM and Redi coefficients.

**Backscatter** (``backscatter``, default off).
:math:`K_u = C_{Ku} \sqrt{2E} \, L_{\mathrm{mix}}` is **subtracted** from
the resolved harmonic viscosity — a deliberate *negative* viscosity that
returns energy to the resolved flow rather than only removing it. It is
floored so the negative mode's growth rate is bounded:

.. math::

   A_{\mathrm{net}} = A_h - K_u ,
   \qquad
   A_{\mathrm{net}} \;\ge\;
     -\,\frac{c_{\mathrm{CFL}}}{2 \Delta t
       \left( \Delta x^{-2} + \Delta y^{-2} \right)} .

.. warning::

   That floor bounds the *growth rate*, not the operator. Backscatter is
   not stable on its own, which is why a biharmonic backstop with a
   **non-zero** dissipation coefficient is mandatory under it, and the
   dispatcher checks for one fail-loud. Selecting a flow-aware biharmonic
   closure whose coefficient still sits at its zero default does **not**
   satisfy the guard.

GM — Gent-McWilliams
--------------------

``&ocean_gm_nml enable``, default off; requires ``&ocean_slopes_nml``.
Parameterizes the adiabatic flattening of isopycnals by unresolved
baroclinic eddies.

The eddy streamfunction is never realised as a velocity. It is realised as
a **thickness flux** folded directly into the layer mass fluxes:

.. math::

   \Psi_{\mathrm{unlim}} \;=\; -\, K_h \, \Delta y_{cu} \, S ,
   \qquad
   S = \text{isopycnal slope} ,

with the per-layer bolus transport the vertical difference of the limited
:math:`\Psi`, accumulated bed-to-surface and closed at the top so that
:math:`\sum_k u^{\mathrm{bolus}}_k = 0` **exactly**. That exact closure is
what makes the parameterization adiabatic: it moves thickness around within
a column without creating or destroying any.

**Slope tapering** is the hard part, because :math:`\Psi \propto S` grows
without bound where isopycnals outcrop. Three limiters apply in order:

.. math::

   \Psi \;=\;
     \frac{\Psi_{\mathrm{unlim}}
           + (S/S_{\max})^2 \, \Psi_{\mathrm{safe}}}
          {1 + (S/S_{\max})^2}

blends toward a column-spread return flow as :math:`|S|` exceeds
``khth_slope_max`` (default 0.01); then a mass-availability bound keeps
every layer above the vanished-layer floor; then a per-layer donor cap
keyed on the transport sign. The coefficient itself is CFL-clamped at
:math:`0.25 \, \texttt{khth\_max\_cfl} / (\Delta t (\Delta x^{-2} +
\Delta y^{-2}))`.

.. note::

   A live divergence from MOM6: the ``nk_linear`` surface linear-return-flow
   region is **not implemented**. Porting it verbatim would place MOM6's
   surface region at Roundabout's seabed, given the inverted layer
   convention. GM answers therefore differ from MOM6's default.

Redi — isoneutral diffusion
---------------------------

``&ocean_redi_nml enable``, default off. Diffuses tracers *along* neutral
surfaces rather than along coordinate surfaces, so that stirring by eddies
does not spuriously mix across density.

.. warning::

   This is **not** an explicit rotated diffusion tensor. Do not read the
   familiar Griffies small-slope tensor into this code — no such tensor is
   assembled anywhere.

What is implemented is the continuous **neutral-diffusion** algorithm.
Phase A locates, for each face, the positions at which the left and right
columns' density profiles are neutrally related, producing a set of neutral
sublayers with effective thicknesses :math:`h_{\mathrm{eff}}`. Phase B then
accumulates the tracer flux over those sublayers,

.. math::

   \mathcal{F} \;=\; \sum_{\text{sublayers}}
     \Delta\theta_{\mathrm{neutral}} \; h_{\mathrm{eff}} \;
     \Delta t \, K_h \, \frac{\Delta y_{cu}}{\Delta x_{cu}} ,

and scatters it back into the native layers. The neutral-surface geometry
is what makes the operator along-isopycnal; the diagonal
:math:`K \nabla_h` part is supplied by the separate along-coordinate tracer
Laplacian it augments.

The discontinuous variant is deferred and is **rejected at configure**.

MLE — mixed-layer restratification
----------------------------------

Fox-Kemper et al. (2008, 2011), ``&ocean_foxkemper_nml enable``, default
off. Submesoscale mixed-layer eddies restratify the surface layer after a
mixing event, and at typical resolutions none of that is resolved.

.. math::
   :label: eq-mle

   \Psi(z) \;=\; C_e \, \frac{H_{ml}^2}{|f|} \,
                 \left( \nabla \bar{b} \times \hat{\mathbf{z}} \right)
                 \mu(z) ,

with :math:`H_{ml}` the mixed-layer depth (from EPBL), :math:`\nabla\bar{b}`
the mixed-layer-mean buoyancy gradient, and :math:`C_e` = ``ce``, default
0.0625. The vertical structure function is FK08 eq. 21,

.. math::

   \mu(\sigma) \;=\; \max\!\left(0, \;
     \bigl( 1 - x^2 \bigr)
     \left( 1 + \tfrac{5}{21} x^2 \right) \right) ,
   \qquad x = 2\sigma + 1 ,

for :math:`\sigma \in [-1, 0]`. Because the per-layer weights are
differences of :math:`\mu` and :math:`\mu(0) - \mu(-1) = 0` exactly, the
result is a **closed overturning cell** — conservative in mass and every
tracer by construction.

Like GM, this is injected as mixed-layer-confined mass transports added to
the layer mass fluxes; the velocity fields are never touched.

The timescale has three forms: the bare
:math:`\tau_s = C_e/\max(|f|, f_{\mathrm{floor}})`, which reproduces FK08
exactly; the FK11 momentum-mixrate form; and the Bodner et al. (2023) form
(``use_bodner``), which introduces an explicit front length scale
:math:`d_s = \sqrt{(\Delta x^2 + \Delta y^2)/2}` and a turbulent velocity
scale, overriding the other two.
