---------------------
Grids and boundaries
---------------------

.. contents::
   :local:


Horizontal grids
================

``&ocean_grid_nml grid_config`` selects the horizontal grid. Accepted
values are ``cartesian`` (the default), ``spherical``, ``supergrid`` and
``tripolar``.

Whichever you choose, the kernels never see it. They consume full
two-dimensional **metric arrays** — cell areas, face widths, inverse
spacings, the Coriolis parameter at corners — from a single metrics slot.
Adding a grid means filling those arrays; it does not mean touching the
dynamical core.

``cartesian``
-------------

A uniform :math:`\Delta x, \Delta y` plane. Set ``dx`` and ``dy`` directly
in ``&grid_nml``, and choose the Coriolis treatment with ``&physics_nml
coriolis_f`` plus ``&ocean_topo_nml coriolis_beta`` for a
:math:`\beta`-plane.

This is the default and the bit-identity reference: every regression
baseline in the repository is a Cartesian run unless it says otherwise.

.. note::

   On a Cartesian grid the geographic latitude field is identically zero.
   Any closure that needs a real latitude fails loud rather than silently
   treating the whole domain as equatorial — the Henyey background mixing
   of :ref:`parameterizations` is the case you are most likely to meet.

``spherical``
-------------

A longitude-latitude sector. ``&ocean_grid_nml`` takes ``lon_west``,
``lat_south``, ``rad_earth`` (default :math:`6.378\times10^{6}\
\mathrm{m}`), and either explicit spacings or the domain extent as
``len_lon`` / ``len_lat`` with ``axis_units = "degrees"``. The Coriolis
parameter comes from the cell latitude; ``coriolis_scheme`` selects
``beta_plane`` (the default) or ``planetary``.

.. warning::

   **Formula-bathymetry length scales are in grid units.** The
   ``&ocean_topo_nml`` seamount and spoon generators work in grid
   coordinates — metres on a Cartesian grid, **degrees** on a spherical or
   curvilinear one. The metres-valued ``slope_scale`` knob is converted at
   the dispatch, but if a raw metres length scale ever reaches a degrees
   position, :math:`\exp(-r^2/L^2) \approx 1` everywhere and the basin
   silently collapses to a flat bottom at the peak depth. It runs; it is
   just not the domain you asked for.

``supergrid``
-------------

Reads a MOM6 mosaic supergrid file, named by ``supergrid_file``. This is
how you bring in a grid generated by the MOM6 tooling rather than
describing one analytically.

``tripolar``
------------

A Murray (1996) bipolar cap. Below ``phi_join`` (default 65°N) the grid is
an ordinary longitude-latitude mesh; above it, the singular North Pole is
replaced by two poles placed over land, so the Arctic is covered without a
converging-meridian singularity in the ocean.

The seam is closed by a **north fold**. Set ``&ocean_bc_nml north =
"tripolar_fold"``; it requires periodic west and east edges, and it is
single-rank. The fold exchange reverses the :math:`i` index across the
seam, flips the sign of vector normals, and projects the duplicated
degrees of freedom on the :math:`v` and corner seam row to be
antisymmetric — the row is represented twice, and the two copies must
remain consistent.

.. note::

   An aquaplanet cannot test a tripolar cap meaningfully. The cap's poles
   are coordinate singularities that a real configuration hides under
   land; with ocean everywhere they are exposed and the resulting
   behaviour tells you about the aquaplanet, not the grid.


Bathymetry
==========

``&ocean_topo_nml topo_config`` selects the bottom. Accepted values are
``flat`` (the default), ``spoon``, ``seamount``, ``neverworld2``,
``island``, ``double_drake`` and ``file``.

The formula generators take ``max_depth``, ``edge_depth`` and
``slope_scale``; ``file`` reads a NetCDF field instead.

.. warning::

   **A formula bathymetry setter must fill the ghost rows.** Leaving
   :math:`h = 0` in the halo makes the EOS fall back to
   :math:`\rho = \rho_0` there, which puts a spurious density jump at every
   wall-adjacent face and blows the run up with an e-folding time of hours.
   The file loader fills ghosts; a new formula path must too.


Land
====

Two mechanisms, for two different problems.

**Static land masking** turns interior cells into free-slip walls by
zeroing the metrics that would carry flux across their faces. There is no
special-casing in the kernels: a face with zero width transports nothing,
and the cell behind it never moves. A land block stays exactly at rest,
and ``test_ocean_land_mask`` asserts precisely that. Skipping the
computation of masked cells entirely is an MPI-level optimisation, not
part of the masking itself.

**Dynamic wetting and drying** (``&ocean_wetdry_nml enable``, default off)
handles a moving shoreline — an intertidal flat, a flooding estuary. Cells
dry below ``dry_depth`` (default 0.05 m) and re-wet above ``rewet_depth``
(default 0.10 m); the hysteresis between the two thresholds prevents a cell
from oscillating. A positive-definite outflow limiter keeps a drying cell
from exporting more water than it contains.

Wet/dry is limited to sigma and z\*-lite coordinates, is single-rank, and
falls outside the ``pred_corr`` outer-scheme envelope — a configuration
enabling it must also set ``&ocean_bt_nml split_scheme = "ssp_rk2"``,
which ``validate_config`` will tell you.


Porous barriers
===============

``&ocean_porous_nml enable``, default off. An Adcroft (2013)
subgrid-blocking treatment for sills and straits: a channel narrower than a
grid cell is invisible to the model, so a per-layer **open-area fraction**
is used to narrow the effective face widths instead.

The fraction comes from a three-parameter fit to the along-face depth
statistics (:math:`d_{\min}`, :math:`d_{\max}`, :math:`d_{\mathrm{avg}}`),
and it narrows the face widths in both the continuity and Coriolis mass
fluxes; the column-integrated fraction narrows the barotropic face widths
too, so the fast loop is not porous-blind. It is recomputed once per outer
step.

``eta_interp`` chooses how the free surface enters the blocking calculation
— ``max`` (the default) blocks the least.

.. note::

   ``source = "resolved"`` is a documented **proxy**, not true subgrid
   information. It derives the statistics from the resolved bathymetry,
   which means it only sees variation *along* the face. A degenerate
   statistic — bathymetry uniform along the face — is deliberately left
   fully open rather than walled at the two-cell mean depth. Genuine
   subgrid blocking needs an offline edge-topography file, and
   ``source = "file"`` currently fails loud.

Porous barriers are single-rank, and are refused alongside a wide-halo
barotropic clone or dynamic wet/dry.


Boundary conditions
===================

``&ocean_bc_nml`` tags each edge independently with the keys ``west``,
``east``, ``south`` and ``north``. Every edge defaults to ``wall``.

.. list-table::
   :header-rows: 1
   :widths: 18 82

   * - Tag
     - Behaviour
   * - ``wall``
     - Closed. Hard zero normal flux. The default.
   * - ``open``
     - Flather radiation. Gravity waves leave the domain instead of
       reflecting; :math:`\eta` is clamped toward a reference value.
   * - ``tidal``
     - Prescribed multi-constituent :math:`\eta`, composed into the same
       reference the Flather condition uses.
   * - ``clamped``
     - Hard Dirichlet on :math:`\eta`, :math:`u`, :math:`v` and every
       tracer.
   * - ``chapman``
     - Orlanski radiation on :math:`\eta` with an implicitly estimated
       phase speed. Carries persistent per-edge state.
   * - ``sponge``
     - A relaxation band. The boundary kernel falls through to ``wall`` at
       the outermost face; the sponge kernel relaxes the interior band
       toward reference values.
   * - ``periodic``
     - Ghost-wrap. **Must be paired** — west with east, south with north —
       and requires ``nghost >= 3`` for the PPM and biharmonic stencils.
       Cannot be combined with ``sponge`` on the same edge.
   * - ``tripolar_fold``
     - The Murray north-fold seam. North edge only; requires periodic west
       and east.
   * - ``nested``, ``inflow``, ``discharge``
     - Accepted by the parser but **reserved** — not implemented.
       ``nested`` behaves like ``open`` to the kernels.

Choosing one
------------

``wall`` is right for a closed basin and for any idealised test where you
want the domain to conserve exactly. It is also the honest default: a wall
cannot silently do the wrong thing.

``periodic`` is right for a channel — a reentrant zonal channel is the
standard configuration for baroclinic-instability and ACC-type problems,
and it removes the wall quantisation of the zonal wavenumber that a closed
basin imposes.

``open`` is what you want at the edge of a regional domain embedded in a
larger ocean, so that waves generated inside leave rather than reflecting
back. Combining it with a ``sponge`` band inside the domain is the usual
practice: the sponge absorbs what the radiation condition does not.

Refinements
-----------

Open edges carry a per-layer zero-gradient baroclinic anomaly on top of the
barotropic Flather mean, plus per-edge tracer inflow values.

``radiation_scheme`` selects ``anomaly`` (the default) or ``orlanski``.
Nudging is asymmetric: ``nudge_tau_in`` and ``nudge_tau_out`` set separate
timescales for inflow and outflow, which is what lets a boundary be firm
about what comes in while staying transparent to what goes out.

Boundary tides pick up the astronomical nodal correction when
``obc_tidal_nodal`` is set, sharing a reference epoch with the interior
body tide so the two stay phase-consistent.

``mask_wall_velocity`` (default ``.true.``) zeroes the wall-normal velocity
in wall ghost cells. Without it, a flat all-wet channel with a wall edge
can carry garbage in the wall-normal component.

A fuller sponge implementation is available separately as
``&ocean_sponge_nml enable``, with per-edge width and strength overrides,
relaxing momentum and every registered tracer toward reference fields.

.. note::

   Per-layer Orlanski phase-speed radiation and file-backed boundary-data
   backends are deferred. The polymorphic update call site is wired into
   the driver, but only the constant backend ships today.
